A rule about what a model tier can or cannot do is calibrated against the
generation it was measured on. When that tier is upgraded the rule is **void** —
it stops applying immediately and is targeted for deletion, not weakened,
reinterpreted, or partly salvaged. Ruled by the user on discovering the loop
still routing by a measurement of **Opus 4.8** ("fear-routing to opus never
cashes out") after Opus 5 had shipped as a frontier thinking-implementor —
stronger than sonnet at implementation and visuals, and near fable in depth.

**Why:** rules of this kind read as timeless engineering principles but are
really bug reports against one model version. Left in place they outlive their
subject and quietly cap the loop at the capability of a retired model — and
because they are *written down*, they get cited as evidence, which is worse than
having no rule at all.

**How to apply:** the tier taxonomy is the durable layer and survives upgrades
([[orchestration-model]]) — fable plans, opus is the thinking implementor,
sonnet the minimum-thinking one, haiku the no-thinking one. Anything layered on
top that names a tier's defects gets a generation stamp and dies with it. On
hearing a model has been upgraded, sweep for rules naming that tier and strike
them. Rules resting on something else — budget (tokens over wall time),
ownership (the orchestrator never implements), licensing, artifact shape — carry
no expiry.
