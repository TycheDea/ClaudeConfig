Research bullets must seek **comparative and experiential** sources — head-to-head
comparisons, arena/ELO leaderboards, practitioner threads, migration write-ups from
people who actually switched — and constrain them by **recency**. Isolated spec-sheet
facts (parameter counts, license tags, "does a loader exist") are necessary but never
sufficient.

**Why:** stated by the user after the A1–A4 AI-pipeline research bullets
recommended image-generation bases without a single sourced quality judgment between
them. Spec facts verify cheaply against primary sources, so I over-indexed on them —
but they answer "is this permissible and does it run", never "is it good and what
breaks when people use it." The real signal lives in how practitioners compare tools.

**How to apply:** every research pass gets a comparative leg alongside the
primary-source verification leg. Weight sources by date — this field turns over in
months, so a comparison one release generation old is worthless; say so explicitly
when only stale comparisons exist. Keep verifying licenses against primary text
([[strict-nc-tooling-ruling]]) — that stops being the whole pass, not a step.
